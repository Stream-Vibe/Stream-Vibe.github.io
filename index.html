TL;DR — Real users rarely state exactly when they need help, and their requests keep changing. StreamVibe studies intent-aware proactive interaction over live video: the agent watches a continuous stream, infers implicit intents, follows intents as they are revised, extended or interrupted, and decides when to speak and what to say.

Abstract

Vision-language models are shifting from offline passive understanding toward online proactive interaction. However, prevailing methods largely rely on explicit requests and miss the implicit user intent (e.g., “where should I click next?” while a user navigates a website), and they fail to update their state as user requests evolve continuously. These limitations make them less suited to intent-aware interaction in practice. To address these challenges, we introduce StreamVibe-Bench, a new benchmark serving as a testbed for user-intent interaction, with 4.1K evaluation samples and 85K training samples spanning explicit, implicit, and dynamic intents. Building on this benchmark, we develop StreamVibe-VL, a streaming proactive model that infers implicit user intents from continuous observations and dynamically tailors its interactions as these intents evolve. We further introduce StreamVibe-Harness, which maintains user intents as persistent states with flexible lifecycles and uses a hierarchical memory to track the latest user intent and environmental state. Our experiments reveal three key findings: (i) StreamVibe-VL preserves strong general video understanding while improving response timing and answer quality by 10.7% over other baselines; (ii) existing proactive models remain limited in understanding user intent and deciding when to respond, especially under implicit and dynamic intents; and (iii) StreamVibe-Harness further improves response consistency and adaptation over sustained interaction, resulting in a 12.3% gain in proactive interaction.

Three levels of user intent

StreamVibe-Bench characterizes proactive interaction along two axes, intent explicitness and intent dynamics.

Level 1

Explicit Intents

The user names a condition, event, or state to monitor; the agent responds when it happens.

“Let me know when the water starts boiling.”
Single EventTransitionRecurring
Level 2

Implicit Intents

Only a high-level goal is given; the agent must infer when and how to assist from what it sees and the task progress.

“Help me keep an eye on this dish.”

Level 3

Dynamic Intents

Intents evolve as the task unfolds; the agent must keep the currently valid set of intents up to date.

StreamVibe-Bench

Task videos from physical (egocentric daily activities, task execution) and digital (navigation, content editing, tool use) environments. Gemini-3.8-Flash proposes help points with response windows and reference replies, then four reviewers independently verify every sample; only samples approved by all reviewers are kept. Annotations are aligned to 1 FPS causal streams.

Pointwise evaluation

Positive and negative timestamps are sampled around annotated help points and each decision is scored independently, with either gold or self-generated history. Isolates local decision ability without error accumulation.

Trajectory evaluation

The model runs over the full stream, every decision conditioned on its own previous responses. Captures error propagation, intent tracking, and repeated or missed responses.

Timing: Precision, Recall, F1, FPR. Quality: Answer Score (0–4, judged) and Content F1 against reference replies.

StreamVibe: grounding evidence, tracking intent

Two challenges fall out of the formulation: the policy must ground each active intent in emerging visual evidence, and someone must track the active intents and interaction history as they evolve. StreamVibe separates the two: a trained streaming policy, StreamVibe-VL, decides when and what to respond; a plug-in StreamVibe-Harness maintains the interaction state it conditions on.

StreamVibe-Harness

  • Interaction state ⟨active intents, task progress, delivered responses⟩, updated only on sufficient evidence.
  • Intent lifecycle: each intent is an independent stateful instance; one-shot intents close once satisfied, persistent ones stay active.
  • Hierarchical memory: recent history kept fine-grained, older history compressed and retrieved by relevance to active intents.

Stage 1 · Adaptive streaming SFT

Decision tokens (silence / response) are re-weighted to the current training distribution while response tokens keep a separate weight, avoiding collapse into always-silent or over-talkative behavior. 70K intent samples mixed with 768K general streaming-video samples.

Stage 2 · Localized trajectory RL

Instead of optimizing whole trajectories with diffuse credit, short causal rollouts are built around help points and optimized with GRPO. Reward R = 0.7·Ralign + 0.3·Rglob: timing×content matching per help point plus trajectory-level coverage, order, consistency and redundancy.

Results

Main results on StreamVibe-Bench (±2 s tolerance). Bold / underline: best / second best within each block.

Streaming models do not support pointwise evaluation and are reported only under trajectory evaluation. P/R/F1/FPR measure response timing; ANS (answer score) and C-F1 (content F1) measure response quality.

RQ1
Stronger proactive interaction, general understanding preserved. StreamVibe-VL beats open-source baselines on StreamVibe-Bench, with the highest Content F1 under trajectory evaluation; RL adds gains over SFT, largest on dynamic intents. It also transfers to EgoProactive and IPIBench and improves over its base model on every general video benchmark.
RQ2
Current proactive agents struggle to stay reliable over time. Implicit and dynamic intents are much harder than explicit ones; higher recall comes with more false triggers; and timing F1 decays with interaction length, so correct individual judgments do not add up to stable behavior.
RQ3
Policy learning and state tracking are complementary. SFT raises sensitivity, RL restores selectivity (lower FPR, better answers), and adding the Harness to a fixed policy further suppresses unnecessary responses, with the clearest gains on dynamic intents.
