01Motivation

Why proactive assistance is hard

Most streaming video assistants respond when a request names the event to watch for, such as “notify me when a goal is scored”. In everyday tasks people usually state a goal instead, and they change their requests as the task goes on. Three problems follow.

1

The intent is often implicit

“Help me finish this whenever I get stuck” names no trigger. The assistant has to decide from the video when help is needed and what kind.

2

The task state keeps changing

Earlier replies and user actions change what is still useful. Without tracking progress, assistants repeat advice, give outdated advice, or miss steps.

3

Requests are revised mid-task

Users add constraints, replace a request, or ask a side question. Assistants tend to keep following the first request.

02Task

Three levels of user intent

StreamVibe-Bench grades proactive interaction by how explicit the intent is and whether it changes during the task.

Level 1 · Explicit

The user names a condition or event; the assistant responds when it occurs.

04StreamVibe-Bench

Benchmark construction and evaluation

Task videos come from physical settings (egocentric daily activities and task execution) and digital settings (navigation, content editing and tool use). Gemini-3.8-Flash proposes candidate help points with response windows and reference replies. Four reviewers check every sample independently, and a sample is kept only if all four approve it. Annotations are aligned to 1 FPS streams.

Pointwise evaluation scores single decisions around help points. Trajectory evaluation runs the model over the whole video with its own reply history.

05Method

StreamVibe-VL and StreamVibe-Harness

StreamVibe has two parts. StreamVibe-VL is a streaming video model that decides at every second whether to respond and what to say. StreamVibe-Harness runs around the model and keeps an explicit record of the active requests, the task progress and the replies already given, which it passes to the model as context.

One second of inference: the Harness updates the interaction state, and the model stays silent until a help point.

HStreamVibe-Harness

The interaction state holds the active requests, the task progress and the replies already delivered. It changes only when the conversation or the video gives enough evidence.

Each request is tracked on its own. One-time requests close once they are satisfied; standing requests stay open. The Harness decides which requests are still valid, but not when to speak.

Recent history is kept in detail and older history is compressed. The parts relevant to the active requests are retrieved for each decision.

1Adaptive streaming SFT

Training sources differ a lot in how often the model should speak. The loss re-weights silence and response decisions to the current training mix and weights reply tokens separately, so the model does not drift toward always staying silent or always talking.

2Localized trajectory RL

Rewarding whole trajectories mixes good and bad decisions. We instead roll out short causal segments around help points and optimize them with GRPO.

The reward is R = 0.7·Ralign + 0.3·Rglob. Ralign scores each matched reply on timing and content; Rglob scores the segment for coverage, order, consistency and redundancy.

Main observations

  1. StreamVibe-VL is the strongest open-source model on StreamVibe-Bench. It has the highest Content F1 at every intent level under trajectory evaluation. RL improves over SFT, most on dynamic intents. The gains carry over to EgoProactive and IPIBench, and accuracy on general video benchmarks goes up rather than down.
  2. Implicit and dynamic intents remain hard for every model. Models that respond more often reach higher recall but also trigger more false alarms, and timing F1 drops as interactions get longer.
  3. Training and state tracking help in different ways. SFT makes the model respond more readily; RL makes it more selective. Adding the Harness to a fixed model removes further unnecessary replies and improves content, with the largest effect on dynamic intents.
07Abstract

Vision-language models are shifting from offline passive understanding toward online proactive interaction. However, prevailing methods largely rely on explicit requests and miss the implicit user intent (e.g., “where should I click next?” while a user navigates a website), and they fail to update their state as user requests evolve continuously. To address these challenges, we introduce StreamVibe-Bench, a benchmark serving as a testbed for user-intent interaction, with 4.1K evaluation samples and 85K training samples spanning explicit, implicit, and dynamic intents. Building on this benchmark, we develop StreamVibe-VL, a streaming proactive model that infers implicit user intents from continuous observations and dynamically tailors its interactions as these intents evolve. We further introduce StreamVibe-Harness, which maintains user intents as persistent states with flexible lifecycles and uses a hierarchical memory to track the latest user intent and environmental state. Our experiments reveal three key findings: (i) StreamVibe-VL preserves strong general video understanding while improving response timing and answer quality by 10.7% over other baselines; (ii) existing proactive models remain limited in understanding user intent and deciding when to respond, especially under implicit and dynamic intents; and (iii) StreamVibe-Harness further improves response consistency and adaptation over sustained interaction, resulting in a 12.3% gain in proactive interaction.
